Mimer vs Maccy, Raycast, Paste & CopyClip

Mimer is a free, open-source macOS clipboard manager built for developers — type-aware history, ⌘K transforms, and history encrypted at rest. Here is how it compares to the ones you have probably heard of.

At a glance

The short version: Maccy is the great free baseline, Raycast bundles clipboard into a launcher, Paste is the polished paid option, and Mimer adds a developer layer — transforms, type-awareness, and encryption — while staying free and open source.

Which one is right for you

Coming from Maccy

Maccy is an excellent, fast, free, open-source baseline — if all you need is history and search, it is hard to beat. Mimer is the step up when you want the clipboard to understand what you copied: ⌘K transforms (JSON → TypeScript, decode a JWT, slugify, Base64), type-aware clips that act on git SHAs and file:line refs, and history that is encrypted at rest rather than stored in the clear.

Using Raycast's clipboard history

Raycast is a launcher with a clipboard feature; it is powerful but closed-source, telemetry-enabled, and its deeper features sit behind Pro. Mimer is the opposite trade: a focused, open-source, local-only tool that does one thing — the clipboard — with developer transforms Raycast's generic history does not.

Paying for Paste, Pastebot, or CopyClip 2

Paste is beautifully polished with iCloud sync, Pastebot has paid paste filters, and CopyClip 2 is the paid sequel to CopyClip — all closed source. Mimer gives you transforms and a paste-stack for free and open source, with encryption and secret masking built in. The one thing it does not do is cross-device sync; that is a deliberate privacy choice.

On Flycut or plain CopyClip

Flycut and CopyClip are free and lightweight but text-only and minimal. Mimer keeps that lightweight, menu-bar-first feel and adds images, type-awareness, transforms, and encryption on top.
